Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2 · Decision for Helen

A missed ping isn't a “no.”

After Release 4.2, four previously reliable responders almost stopped being asked. The evidence points to a scoring rule that nobody chose for this, though two dependencies still need confirming. Here's what I'd do about it.

1

Stop counting misses as turn-downs

A ping that runs out just moves to the next responder. It no longer costs the responder −0.12 the way a “no” does. Whether a run of misses should cost anything is still open (D2).

2

Recalculate every score by rule

One recorded replay of everyone's ping history under the new rule. There's no hand-picked list and no repeatable “reset” button.

3

Show handlers why someone is quiet

Each console card says what's happening, why, and what to do. I recommend asking “still available?” after 3 misses in a row (D2), plus an “isn't being asked” alert whose threshold is still open (D8).

Keep the proximity change. 4.2 already cut acceptance weight from 0.40 to 0.25, so reverting it wouldn't fix this. Leave the 60s ping wait as the next decision. Before any code: Wen checks nothing else caps the list (D1), Ravi confirms the numbers, and Helen signs off.

# history.py
def record_accepted(responder):
    """They took the callout. Score goes up."""
    _set(responder, recent_acceptance(responder) + ACCEPTANCE_CREDIT)   # +0.08

# TODO(2019): should this ease back toward NEUTRAL_SCORE on its own...?
# Leaving it as-is for now.
def record_declined(responder):
    """They turned it down, or we ran out of time waiting.
    Score goes down. Same either way ..."""
    _set(responder, recent_acceptance(responder) - DECLINE_PENALTY)    # −0.12
